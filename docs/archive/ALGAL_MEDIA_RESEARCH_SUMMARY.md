# Algal Culture Media Research Summary

This document provides comprehensive information on common algal culture media and stock solutions, compiled from authoritative sources including UTEX, CCAP, NCMA, and peer-reviewed literature.

## Complete Media

### 1. BG-11 Medium

**Purpose**: Freshwater, soil, thermal, and marine cyanobacteria culture

**Key Characteristics**:
- Derived from medium no. 11 (Hughes et al. 1958)
- Modified by Allen and Stanier (1968)
- One of the most widely used cyanobacteria media
- Contains high phosphate levels and micronutrients

**Major Components** (per liter):
1. NaNO₃ (sodium nitrate) - nitrogen source
2. K₂HPO₄ (dipotassium phosphate) - phosphorus source
3. MgSO₄·7H₂O (magnesium sulfate) - magnesium source
4. CaCl₂·2H₂O (calcium chloride) - calcium source
5. Na₂CO₃ (sodium carbonate) - carbon source
6. Citric acid - chelating agent
7. Ferric ammonium citrate - iron source
8. EDTA (disodium salt) - chelating agent
9. BG-11 Trace Metals Solution (1 mL/L)
10. Sodium thiosulfate (optional, for agar media)

**Preparation**:
- pH typically adjusted to 7.0-7.5 before sterilization
- Autoclave at 15 psi for 15 minutes
- Store at refrigerator temperature
- Allow 24 hours for gaseous equilibrium before inoculation

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Allen, M.M. and Stanier, R.Y. (1968)

### 2. Bold 1NV Medium

**Purpose**: General purpose freshwater medium for xenic cultures

**Key Characteristics**:
- Modification of Bold's Basal Medium recipe
- Suitable for various algae classes
- Contains vitamins for enhanced growth

**Major Components** (per liter):
1. NaNO₃ - 10 mL/L (2.94 mM final)
2. CaCl₂·2H₂O - 10 mL/L (0.17 mM final)
3. MgSO₄·7H₂O - 10 mL/L (0.3 mM final)
4. K₂HPO₄ - 10 mL/L (0.43 mM final)
5. KH₂PO₄ - phosphorus buffer
6. NaCl - sodium source
7. Bold Trace Stock Solution
8. Vitamins (thiamine, biotin, B12)

**Preparation**:
- Add components to 900 mL dH₂O while stirring
- Bring to 1 L total volume
- Autoclave for liquid media
- For agar: add vitamins after autoclaving before solidification

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Bold, H.C. (1949)

---

### 3. Bristol Medium

**Purpose**: General purpose freshwater medium, especially for green algae (Chlorophyta)

**Key Characteristics**:
- Essential component used in other media formulations
- Simple, reliable formulation
- Commonly used for laboratory algae cultivation
- Bristol's solution is often a base for modified media

**Applications**:
- Green algae (Chlorophyta) cultures
- General phycology research
- Teaching laboratories

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Cyanosite Growth Media database

---

### 4. Modified Bold 3N Medium

**Purpose**: General purpose freshwater medium for xenic cultures, especially blue-greens and reds

**Key Characteristics**:
- Contains 3-fold nitrogen compared to standard Bold's
- pH 6.2 (acidic compared to most algal media)
- Suitable for cyanobacteria and red algae

**Preparation Notes**:
- Add components to 850 mL dH₂O while stirring
- Bring to 1 L total volume
- pH adjustment before sterilization
- Wait for first component to dissolve before adding next
- For agar: add 15 g agar without mixing, autoclave, then add vitamins when cooled

**Storage**: Refrigerator temperature

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- CCAP media recipes

---

### 5. F/2 Medium

**Purpose**: Coastal marine algae, especially diatoms

**Key Characteristics**:
- Developed by Guillard and Ryther (1962), modified by Guillard (1975)
- Original "f Medium" concentration reduced by half (hence "F/2")
- One of the most common marine algae media worldwide
- Based on enriched seawater

**Major Components** (per liter of filtered seawater):
1. NaNO₃ (sodium nitrate) - 75 mg/L
2. NaH₂PO₄·H₂O (sodium phosphate) - 5 mg/L
3. Na₂SiO₃·9H₂O (sodium metasilicate) - 30 mg/L (for diatoms)
4. F/2 Trace Metals Solution
5. F/2 Vitamins Solution

**Preparation**:
- Begin with 950 mL filtered natural seawater (or artificial seawater)
- Add nutrient stock solutions
- Add trace metal and vitamin solutions
- Bring to 1 L with filtered seawater
- Filter sterilize or autoclave carefully

**References**:
- NCMA (National Center for Marine Algae and Microbiota)
- Guillard, R.R.L. and Ryther, J.H. (1962)
- Guillard, R.R.L. (1975)

### 6. Allen Medium

**Purpose**: Freshwater cyanobacteria (blue-green algae), axenic and xenic cultures

**Key Characteristics**:
- Modification of M.M. Allen's medium
- Contains HEPES buffer for pH stability
- NOT typically used for Volvox (despite common confusion)

**Major Components** (per liter):
1. HEPES buffer - 2.3 g/L (0.01 M)
2. NaNO₃ - 1.5 g/L (0.018 M)
3. K₂HPO₄ - 5 mL/L (0.215 mM final from stock)
4. MgSO₄·7H₂O
5. P-IV Metal Solution - 1 mL/L

**Preparation**:
- Add components to 950 mL dH₂O in order while stirring
- Adjust pH to 7.8
- Bring to 1 L total volume
- Autoclave and store at refrigerator temperature

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Allen, M.M. (1968)

### 7. Volvox Medium

**Purpose**: Volvox species and related colonial green algae

**Key Characteristics**:
- Specialized for Volvox cultivation
- May include organic nutrients
- Often used with MES buffer (MES-Volvox Medium)

**Related Media**:
- MES-Volvox Medium: for strains requiring ammonium
- Volvox-Dextrose Medium: for heterotrophic growth
- Standard Volvox Medium: basic formulation

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols

### 8. Waris Medium

**Purpose**: Desmids (xenic cultures)

**Major Components** (per liter):
1. KNO₃ - 1 mL/L (0.99 mM final from 10 g/100 mL stock)
2. MgSO₄·7H₂O - 1 mL/L (0.081 mM final from 2 g/100 mL stock)
3. (NH₄)₂HPO₄ - 1 mL/L (0.151 mM final from 2 g/100 mL stock)
4. CaSO₄·2H₂O - 30 mL/L (0.35 mM final from 0.2 g/100 mL stock)
5. P-IV Metal Solution - 6 mL/L

**Preparation**:
- Add components to 900 mL dH₂O in order while stirring
- Adjust pH to 6.0
- Bring to 1 L total volume
- Autoclave

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols

### 9. Euglena Medium

**Purpose**: Euglena gracilis and Chlorogonium species (axenic cultures)

**Key Characteristics**:
- Heterotrophic medium with organic nutrients
- Contains acetate as carbon source
- Rich medium with protein sources

**Standard Composition (EG:JM)** (per liter):
1. Sodium acetate trihydrate - 1.0 g/L
2. Lab-Lemco powder - 1.0 g/L
3. Tryptone - 2.0 g/L
4. Yeast extract - 2.0 g/L
5. CaCl₂ stock solution - 1.0 mL (from 1.0 g/100 mL stock)

**Alternative (Cramer and Myers CM Medium)**:
1. (NH₄)₂HPO₄ - 1 g/L
2. KH₂PO₄ - 1 g/L
3. MgSO₄·7H₂O - 0.2 g/L
4. CaCl₂·2H₂O - 0.02 g/L
5. Trace elements (Fe, Mn, Co)
6. Vitamins (thiamine, cyanocobalamin)

**Preparation**:
- Add components to 950 mL distilled water while stirring
- Wait for complete dissolution between additions
- Bring to 1 L total volume
- Autoclave at 15 psi for 15 minutes

**References**:
- CCAP media recipes
- Cramer and Myers (1952)

### 10. Erdschreiber's Medium

**Purpose**: General purpose marine medium for xenic cultures

**Key Characteristics**:
- Modified from Plymouth seawater recipe
- Available in multiple dilutions (1/4, 1/2, 1X, 2X)
- Contains soil water extract for growth factors
- Various salinity options for different marine algae

**Components** (per 3 L):
1. Pasteurized seawater (30 ppt)
2. P-IV Metal Solution - 36 mL/3L
3. NaNO₃ - 10 mL/3L (2.3 mM final from 0.7 M stock)
4. Na₂HPO₄·7H₂O - 10 mL/3L (0.067 mM final from 0.02 M stock)
5. Soilwater: GR+ Medium - 150 mL/3L
6. Vitamin B₁₂ - 3 mL/3L

**Variations**:
- 1/4 Erdschreiber's: 7.5 ppt salinity
- 1/2 Erdschreiber's: 15 ppt salinity
- 2X Erdschreiber's: 60 ppt salinity (high salinity strains)

**Preparation**:
- Aseptically add each sterile component to pasteurized seawater
- Add in specified order
- No autoclaving after mixing (components pre-sterilized)

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Plymouth Marine Laboratory methods

### 11. DYIII Medium

**Purpose**: Freshwater chrysophytes and cryptomonads, some dinoflagellates (xenic cultures)

**Key Characteristics**:
- Modification of Sandgren's recipe
- Defined medium often supplemented with vitamins
- Contains MES buffer for pH stability

**Major Components**:
1. MES buffer
2. Metal solutions
3. CaCl₂ (calcium chloride)
4. MgSO₄ (magnesium sulfate)
5. Sodium glycerophosphate
6. Vitamins (when added)

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Lehman (1976)
- Biocyclopedia algae culture methods

---

### 12. DYV Medium

**Purpose**: Freshwater chrysophytes and synurophytes (xenic cultures)

**Key Characteristics**:
- Modification of Andersen's recipe based on DYIII
- Primary medium for NCMA freshwater strains
- Similar to DYIII with addition of DYV Metal Solution

**Differences from DYIII**:
- Contains 1 mL DYV Metal Solution (additional trace elements)
- Developed by Keller and Andersen for improved chrysophyte growth

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- NCMA (National Center for Marine Algae and Microbiota)
- Andersen (2005)

---

### 13. Modified COMBO Medium

**Purpose**: Freshwater algae and zooplankton co-culture

**Key Characteristics**:
- Based on WC medium (Guillard and Lorenzen 1972)
- Enriched with animal trace metals
- Supports both algae and zooplankton without modification
- Originally designed for softwater Daphnia, modified for hardwater species

**Applications**:
- Cyanobacteria cultures
- Cryptophytes
- Green algae
- Diatoms
- Daphnia and other cladoceran zooplankton

**Key Components**:
- Algal macronutrients
- Algal Trace Elements (ATE) solution
- Animal Trace Elements (ANIMATE) solution
- Vitamins (VIM) solution

**Notable Features**:
- No glycylglycine or Tris buffer (unlike WC)
- Animal trace elements necessary for zooplankton but not algae
- Supports continuous co-culture systems

**References**:
- Kilham et al. (1998) Hydrobiologia
- NCMA COMBO medium protocol
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols

---

### 14. Beijerinck's Solution

**Purpose**: Basic nutrient medium component for various algal classes

**Composition** (per liter):
1. NH₄Cl (ammonium chloride) - 8 g/L (7.5 mM final)
2. CaCl₂·2H₂O (calcium chloride) - 1 g/L (0.35 mM final)
3. MgSO₄·7H₂O (magnesium sulfate) - 2 g/L (0.4 mM final)

**Key Characteristics**:
- Defined medium proven successful for many algal classes
- Used as component in complex media (e.g., TAP Medium uses 100 mL/2L)
- Provides essential minerals: nitrogen, calcium, magnesium
- Named after Martinus Beijerinck (pioneer microbiologist)

**Applications**:
- Chlorella vulgaris Beijerinck cultivation
- Component of TAP (Tris Acetate Phosphate) Medium
- General freshwater algae cultivation

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Beijerinck, M.W. (historical)

### 2. Bold Trace Stock Solution (BBM Trace Stock)

**Purpose**: Micronutrient supplement for Bold's Basal Medium and variants

**Composition** (per liter):
1. Concentrated H₂SO₄ - 10 mL (added first to aid dissolution)
2. ZnSO₄·7H₂O (zinc sulfate) - 8.82 g
3. MnCl₂·4H₂O (manganese chloride) - 1.44 g
4. MoO₃ (molybdenum trioxide) - 0.71 g
5. CuSO₄·5H₂O (copper sulfate) - 1.57 g
6. Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O (cobalt nitrate) - 0.49 g

**Preparation**:
- Add concentrated sulfuric acid FIRST to ~900 mL dH₂O
- Add metals in order while stirring continuously
- Bring to 1 L total volume
- Store at room temperature in darkness

**Safety Note**: Strong acid - use appropriate PPE and fume hood

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Bold, H.C. (1949)

### 3. F/2 Vitamins Solution

**Purpose**: Vitamin supplement for F/2 and related marine media

**Composition** (per liter):
1. Thiamine HCl (vitamin B₁) - 200 mg/L
2. Biotin (vitamin H) - 0.5 mg/L (from 0.1 g/L stock, add 10 mL)
3. Cyanocobalamin (vitamin B₁₂) - 0.5 mg/L (from 1.0 g/L stock, add 1 mL)

**Preparation**:
- Dissolve thiamine in 950 mL distilled water
- Add biotin and cyanocobalamin stocks as indicated
- Bring to 1 L total volume
- Filter sterilize (heat-labile vitamins)
- Store in dark at freezer temperature

**Usage**: Typically 1 mL per liter of medium

**References**:
- NCMA F/2 medium protocol
- Guillard (1975)
- Canadian Phycological Culture Centre

### 11. Trace Metals Solution (Generic)

**Purpose**: General-purpose trace metal supplement

### 24. Chelated Iron Solution

**Purpose**: Iron supplement with EDTA chelation for algae culture

**Standard Formulation**:
- **Industry Standard**: Fe-EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid ferric sodium salt)
- Maintains iron in bioavailable form
- Prevents precipitation in culture media

**Preparation Method**:
- Component A: EDTA dissolved in water
- Component B: 0.81 g FeCl₃·6H₂O in 0.1 M HCl, brought to 500 mL
- Combine components and adjust to final volume

**Key Principle**:
- EDTA⁴⁻ wraps around Fe³⁺ ion
- Binds through 4 carboxylates and 2 amines
- Near neutral pH: [Fe(EDTA)(H₂O)]⁻ complex
- Without chelation: ferric ions precipitate as insoluble hydroxides

**Alternative Chelators** (research ongoing):
- Biodegradable chelators for environmental sustainability
- EDTA is persistent in environment

**References**:
- UTEX Culture Collection protocols
- Sigma-Aldrich plant tissue culture protocols
- Journal of Applied Phycology (chelator research)

### 26. EDTA Stock Solution

**Purpose**: Chelating agent stock for preparing trace metal solutions

**Standard Concentrations**:
1. **0.5 M EDTA (pH 8.0)** - common molecular biology stock
2. **BBM EDTA Stock**: ~50 g/L (0.135 M)
3. **Fe-EDTA Stock**: 1.7 mM (0.66 g/L Na₂EDTA·2H₂O)

**Preparation**:
- Maximum solubility at pH 8 is ~0.5 M
- Lower pH = lower solubility
- Higher pH = higher solubility
- Add NaOH to dissolve and adjust pH

**Important Considerations**:
- **Excess EDTA** (>400 mg/L) can inhibit:
  - Cell division
  - Chlorophyll synthesis
  - Algal biomass production
- Balance EDTA concentration with trace element content

**Chemical Forms**:
- EDTA acid (free acid form)
- Na₂EDTA (disodium salt)
- Na₂EDTA·2H₂O (disodium salt dihydrate) - most common

## General Preparation Guidelines

### Sterilization Methods

1. **Autoclaving**:
   - Standard: 121°C, 15 psi, 15-20 minutes
   - For agar media: 15 g agar per liter
   - Allow 24 hours for CO₂ equilibrium in unbuffered media

2. **Filter Sterilization**:
   - Heat-sensitive vitamins
   - 0.22 µm membrane filters
   - Add aseptically to cooled, autoclaved media

3. **Pasteurization**:
   - Seawater: 95°C for 1 hour
   - Avoids precipitation of salts

### Storage Guidelines

1. **Complete Media**:
   - Refrigerator temperature (4°C)
   - Dark (light degrades some nutrients)
   - Use within 1-3 months

2. **Stock Solutions**:
   - Trace metals: Refrigerator (4°C), dark
   - Vitamins: Freezer (-20°C), dark
   - EDTA solutions: Room temperature, dark
   - Divided into aliquots to avoid freeze-thaw cycles

3. **Agar Media**:
   - Refrigerator in sealed containers
   - Use within 1 month
   - Watch for contamination

### pH Adjustment

- Adjust before sterilization for most media
- Use NaOH or HCl (not bicarbonate - releases CO₂)
- Autoclaving can change pH (especially unbuffered media)
- Measure and record final pH

### Quality Control

1. **Check for contamination** before inoculation
2. **Verify pH** of final medium
3. **Test with known strains** before using with valuable cultures
4. **Document batch numbers** and preparation dates
5. **Record any deviations** from standard protocols

## Notes for Media Preparation

### Critical Success Factors

1. **Water Quality**: Use deionized, distilled, or RO water (not tap water)
2. **Chemical Quality**: Use reagent grade or higher purity chemicals
3. **Order of Addition**: Follow specified order (especially EDTA before metals)
4. **Stirring**: Continuous stirring prevents precipitation
5. **pH Control**: Adjust before sterilization for stability
6. **Contamination Prevention**: Use aseptic technique throughout

### Common Problems and Solutions

1. **Precipitation**:
   - Ensure EDTA added before metals
   - Check pH (metals precipitate at high pH)
   - Sterilize some components separately

2. **Poor Growth**:
   - Check vitamin freshness (degrade over time)
   - Verify trace metal concentrations
   - Test water quality
   - Confirm correct pH

3. **Contamination**:
   - Improve aseptic technique
   - Check autoclave function
   - Use fresh stock solutions
   - Consider antibiotic treatment (if appropriate)

### Adaptations for Different Species

- **High light species**: May need increased nutrient concentrations
- **Low light species**: Reduce nutrients to prevent toxicity
- **Calcifying species**: Ensure adequate calcium and pH buffering
- **Nitrogen fixers**: Can omit or reduce nitrate (use BG-11-N)
- **Diatoms**: Must include silica source (sodium metasilicate)
- **Marine species**: Use natural or artificial seawater as base
